Arrively Review (2026): Drive-Time Scheduling for Mobile Detailers

Arrively is a mobile-service scheduling platform built around a problem generic calendars often miss: the time it takes to drive between customers. For mobile detailers with flat-rate packages, its strongest case is self-booking that respects travel time, automatic cancellation gap-fill and a field-first mobile workflow.

Research status: This is a research-based review using Arrively's published product, pricing and mobile-detailing documentation. DetailerFit has not claimed hands-on testing. How we review software.
Commercial relationship: DetailerFit and Arrively are discussing a potential future referral partnership. No paid referral terms or affiliate link are active on this page as of September 26, 2026. The relationship does not determine our conclusions.

Quick verdict

Best fit: mobile detailers who sell relatively standardized packages and lose time to inefficient travel, back-and-forth booking or last-minute cancellations.

Less suitable: shops that need a full automotive CRM with line-item estimates, invoicing, VIN scanning, vehicle-history workflows or deep reconditioning documentation. Arrively positions itself as a focused scheduler rather than an all-in-one detailing operating system.

What Arrively does differently

Most appointment tools can block a two-hour detail. Arrively's more specific promise is that the available slot also accounts for the drive from the prior appointment and to the next one. Customers are shown only slots the operator can realistically reach inside the configured service area.

That matters for a one-van detailer because an apparently full calendar can still be inefficient if the route zigzags across town. Arrively also publishes a cancellation gap-fill workflow that identifies geographically nearby customers with future appointments and offers them an earlier opening.

Features that matter for mobile detailing

  • Drive-time-aware scheduling: real travel time is built into availability.
  • Customer self-booking: branded booking links show reachable slots.
  • Service areas: operators can set a travel radius and availability.
  • Cancellation gap-fill: nearby future customers can be offered an open slot automatically.
  • Recurring appointments: weekly, biweekly and 3–8 week cadences are published for maintenance-style work.
  • Notifications: booking confirmations, reminders, on-the-way ETA and completion updates via SMS/email.
  • Mobile apps: iOS and Android apps with offline access, job status updates and booking-link creation.
  • Payments: Stripe, card-on-file at booking and tap-to-pay.
  • Team support: technician schedules and service areas; Pro publishes unlimited users.

Arrively pricing for detailers

The pricing model is unusual compared with flat per-seat SaaS. Every account starts with 20 free jobs. Low-volume operators can then use pay-as-you-go at $0.49 per job with one user and no monthly fee. Growth is $49/month with 150 jobs included, $0.25 per additional job and three users. Pro is $149/month with 600 jobs included, $0.15 per additional job and unlimited users.

This can fit seasonal or growing mobile businesses, but compare the effective monthly cost at your real job volume rather than only the headline price.

Where Arrively is weaker than a detailing CRM

Arrively's focus is also its main limitation. Its own comparison material says it does not provide the quoting, invoicing and full CRM pipeline found in broader field-service platforms such as Jobber. For detailers, that means you should not assume features such as line-item estimates, VIN scanning, CARFAX history, paint-condition workflows or automotive-specific inspections are included.

Decision rule: If travel time and booking friction are your biggest operational problems, Arrively is unusually focused. If estimating, invoicing, vehicle records and shop workflow are the bigger problem, compare a fuller detailing CRM first.

Setup and migration

Setup Difficulty: Low–Moderate. Arrively markets setup in minutes, with availability, service radius, services and booking links as the core configuration. That sounds lighter than migrating into a large CRM, but DetailerFit has not independently timed setup.

Migration Difficulty: Verify before switching. If you already have recurring clients, future appointments, customer notes or a large CRM database, confirm exactly what can be imported and what must be recreated. Migration support should be treated as a purchase-stage question rather than assumed from the simple onboarding flow.

Arrively vs Jobber for a mobile detailer

Arrively: stronger fit when route-aware self-booking, gap-fill and a focused mobile schedule are the main buying criteria.

Jobber: stronger fit when you want a broader quote → job → invoice → payment workflow and deeper general field-service CRM in one system.

Neither is automatically the better choice. The deciding question is whether your bottleneck is calendar geography or full business administration.

What DetailerFit would verify in a hands-on test

  • How accurately reachable slots react to real traffic and schedule changes.
  • How much manual control an operator retains over route order.
  • How gap-fill behaves when several nearby customers are eligible.
  • How offline edits reconcile after reconnecting.
  • Whether customer/service imports are practical for an established detailer.
  • How Stripe payment handling and refunds feel in a real job workflow.

Bottom line

Arrively is one of the more differentiated scheduling products DetailerFit has found for a genuinely mobile operator because it treats travel time as part of the appointment instead of an afterthought. It is not a replacement for every detailing CRM. For flat-rate mobile detailers whose day is constrained by geography, however, the narrow focus is exactly why it belongs on the shortlist.
