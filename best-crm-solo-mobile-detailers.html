What a solo mobile detailing CRM actually needs

For a one-person detailing business, more features are not automatically better. Every extra subscription dollar and every extra setup step has to earn its place.

We would prioritize six things:

  • Customer records: one place for names, vehicles, contact history and job information.
  • Quotes and estimates: fast enough to use from your phone between jobs.
  • Scheduling: a clear calendar that reduces double-booking and forgotten appointments.
  • Invoices and payments: move from completed job to paid job with minimal friction.
  • Follow-up: reminders and customer communication that do not require another app.
  • Room to grow: you should not need to migrate your entire business the moment you hire your first technician.

Features such as advanced dispatch, employee GPS, sales commissions and complex pipelines can be valuable later. For a solo operator, paying for them before you need them can simply increase overhead.

2. ServiceM8 — best for an iPhone-first solo detailer

ServiceM8 has unusually strong entry economics. Its U.S. pricing currently publishes a Free plan at $0/month for one user and up to 30 new jobs per month. Starter costs $29/month, allows unlimited users and supports up to 50 new jobs per month.

For a solo operator, the free tier gives you a low-risk way to test whether structured job-management software actually improves your day.

The catch is device strategy. ServiceM8 explicitly says it optimizes for Apple devices and recommends iPhone or iPad for the full experience. Android field users can use ServiceM8 Lite for basic functionality.

Choose ServiceM8 if

  • You already run the business from an iPhone or iPad.
  • You want to start at $0 or around $29/month.
  • Your monthly new-job volume fits the plan limits.
  • You expect to add users and like the unlimited-user structure on paid plans.

3. Jobber — best if online booking matters immediately

Jobber is a broader field-service platform rather than detailing-specific software, but its entry plan has one major advantage for a solo operator: online booking is included on Core.

For one user, current pricing is $49/month with no commitment or $29/month when billed annually. Core also publishes quotes, invoices, online payments, reporting and a website alongside scheduling and customer management.

If you are trying to reduce the number of “What times are you available?” messages you handle manually, online booking can be worth paying for even when another CRM has a cheaper monthly headline price.

Choose Jobber if

  • You want customers to book online from the beginning.
  • You want a mature general field-service platform.
  • You may expand beyond detailing into other mobile services.
  • You are comfortable with annual billing to reach the lowest published one-user price.

4. Urable — best if solo is temporary

Urable starts higher at $70/month for Express, but it is particularly interesting for detailers who expect to add employees.

How much should a solo detailer spend on CRM software?

There is no universal percentage because detailing businesses have different ticket sizes, job counts and margins. A better rule is to measure the software against the bottleneck it removes.

If a $30–$80 monthly tool saves several hours of admin, prevents missed follow-ups or helps convert one additional profitable job, it can justify itself quickly. If you barely use the features, even a $29 subscription is wasted overhead.

Simple buying rule: Start with the cheapest plan that solves your current bottleneck. Upgrade because your workflow requires it—not because the feature list looks impressive.

Do you actually need a CRM when you are solo?

Not necessarily on day one. If you only handle a few jobs each month, a calendar, payment processor and organized contact list can work.

The case for CRM software gets stronger when you notice the same problems repeating: leads disappear in text threads, quotes are slow, appointments need manual confirmation, customer history is hard to find, invoices are delayed or you spend evenings doing admin instead of finishing the workday.

At that point, the CRM is not primarily a database. It becomes the operating layer between lead → quote → appointment → job → invoice → payment → repeat customer.

Our final recommendation

For a genuinely solo mobile detailer who wants to move from scattered tools into one system without taking on a large monthly bill, QuoteIQ Essentials is our research-based starting pick at $29.99/month.

The best CRM is the one that removes the most expensive friction from the way your detailing business actually runs.
