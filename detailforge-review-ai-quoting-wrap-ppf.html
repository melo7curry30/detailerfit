DETAILFORGE Review (2026): AI 3D Quoting for Wrap, PPF & Tint Shops

DETAILFORGE is attacking a part of the sales process that most automotive appearance CRMs barely touch: helping a customer see the build before buying it. The platform combines a browser-based 3D vehicle configurator, conversational AI quoting, a live materials and services catalog, online booking, payments, CRM and shop operations. That makes it one of the more unusual new tools for wrap, PPF, tint and ceramic-coating businesses — but it is also early enough that shops should separate the product vision from what has been proven in day-to-day production.

What Is DETAILFORGE?

DETAILFORGE is an all-in-one business platform built specifically for automotive appearance shops offering services such as vehicle wraps, paint protection film, window tint, ceramic coating and detailing.

The platform combines two layers that are normally separated:

  • Customer-facing sales tools: 3D vehicle configuration, AI-assisted quoting, finish selection, shareable quotes and online booking.
  • Shop operations: CRM, jobs, work orders, scheduling, deposits, payments, invoicing, materials, reminders, inspections, waivers, team management and reporting.

The core idea is straightforward: build the shop's service catalog once, including its own pricing, labor assumptions, finishes and materials, then use that same source of truth across quotes, booking and operations.

That architecture matters because one of the most common problems in an appearance shop is duplicated pricing logic. The website says one thing, the sales rep remembers another, the quote template uses an older price and the job board has to be updated manually. DETAILFORGE is designed around keeping those surfaces tied to one catalog.

Why the 3D + AI Workflow Matters

For a standard wash or maintenance detail, visual configuration is probably unnecessary. For a color-change wrap, PPF package, tint combination or appearance upgrade, the buying process is different: customers often need help deciding what they want before they are ready to buy.

DETAILFORGE's differentiator is that the customer can describe a desired build in plain English, while the system uses the shop's catalog and labor rates to produce a priced configuration. The vendor also says customers can rotate a 3D preview, browse finishes and receive AI-rendered visualizations.

The real opportunity is conversion, not novelty

A 3D configurator is only valuable if it shortens the path from “I am thinking about a wrap” to “here is the exact package I want, here is the deposit, book me.” That is the metric shops should test.

This is why DETAILFORGE is strategically different from a conventional CRM. A CRM usually records and manages demand. DETAILFORGE is trying to shape demand before the quote is finalized.

What Each Plan Actually Unlocks

Solo — $79/month

Solo already contains most of the product's headline idea: the 3D configurator, AI assistant, five included AI renders per month, jobs, work orders, calendar, quotes, invoices, card payments, deposits, tips, customer and vehicle records, stock tracking and automated reminders.

For a one-person or very small operation, this is the plan to test the concept. The limitation is that customer self-booking and website embedding are not listed until Pro.

Pro — $149/month

Pro is the more important commercial tier. It increases the included AI renders to 25 per month and adds:

  • customer online booking with real availability and deposits;
  • website embedding for the configurator;
  • automated workflows for reminders, follow-up and deposit chasing;
  • commission tracking, timesheets and payroll exports;
  • photo/video inspections;
  • per-technician task assignment;
  • QuickBooks synchronization;
  • job-level margin reporting;
  • documents and e-signature waivers;
  • a dispatch board with route optimization for mobile work.

For a shop evaluating DETAILFORGE as a genuine operating system rather than a quoting experiment, Pro is the plan we would benchmark.

Enterprise — $279/month

Enterprise raises the included AI-render allowance to 40 per month and adds multi-shop management, white-label customer surfaces, a central catalog for multiple locations, API keys, signed webhooks, calendar feeds and the option to bring your own AI-provider keys.

That is more relevant to multi-location groups, franchises, agencies or technically sophisticated shops than to the average independent detailer.

AI Quoting and Catalog Logic

The strongest part of DETAILFORGE's product story is not “AI” by itself. It is that the AI is supposed to operate against the shop's own structured catalog.

A shop can define services, pricing, coverage, finishes, materials and labor rules, then use the same catalog across multiple surfaces. The vendor says a shop can also describe a service in natural language and let the AI draft the service structure before the owner confirms it.

For customer-facing quoting, the AI assistant can interpret a request such as a customer wanting a specific panel wrapped in a specific finish, then price that build from the configured shop data.

This is more useful than a generic chatbot if it works reliably because the important question is not whether an AI can write a friendly reply. It is whether the system can move from intent → valid service configuration → correct price → deposit → booked job without the owner re-entering everything.

CRM, Booking, Payments and Shop Operations

DETAILFORGE is broader than its 3D configurator. The current product page also publishes a substantial back-office stack:

  • CRM: customer records, vehicle garage, segments, campaigns, coupons and referrals.
  • Scheduling: shop calendar, booking, waitlist, reminders and mobile dispatch.
  • Jobs: stages, checklists, photos, inspections, waivers and warranty tracking.
  • Payments: card payments, deposits, tips, refunds, gift cards and fleet billing through Stripe infrastructure.
  • Messaging: two-way SMS tied to customer/job context.
  • Materials: catalog-based stock tracking, reservations and low-stock alerts.
  • Team: permissions, timesheets, overtime policy, commissions and payroll exports.
  • Reporting: job margins, labor versus material cost and payment reporting.

The vendor also lists native QuickBooks synchronization. Square is described as coming soon, while Zapier and custom CRM workflows currently rely on the platform's API and webhooks rather than a simple native Zapier listing.

DETAILFORGE vs Tint Wiz

Tint Wiz is the more established film-business operating system and has strong workflows around professional proposals, film-roll inventory, projects, reminders and both automotive and flat-glass work. DETAILFORGE's bet is different: it wants the visual configuration and AI-assisted sale to be part of the operating system itself.

Choose Tint Wiz if proven tint/film workflow depth and inventory control matter more. Test DETAILFORGE if the shop sells visually complex, high-ticket upgrades and wants the quoting experience itself to become a conversion tool.

DETAILFORGE vs QuoteIQ

QuoteIQ starts much cheaper and now includes a broad set of AI tools, including natural-language CRM control, photo-based estimation and AI phone functionality through its credit system. It is designed for home-service contractors more broadly, while also marketing directly to mobile detailing businesses.

DETAILFORGE is more narrowly aligned with automotive appearance and has the stronger visual-configuration story. QuoteIQ has the stronger low-cost entry point and broader AI front-office toolkit.

DETAILFORGE vs OmniPlot

OmniPlot solves a different problem. It is cutting software: pattern selection, nesting, export and plotter control. DETAILFORGE is sales and shop-management software.

A PPF shop could realistically use both: DETAILFORGE to configure, quote, book and manage the customer, then dedicated pattern software to prepare the actual film cut.

Do not make the category mistake

DETAILFORGE showing a customer a PPF or tint configuration does not mean it replaces production pattern software. Visual quoting and pattern cutting are separate jobs.

What DETAILFORGE Does Not Replace

The platform's all-in-one positioning is broad, but a shop should be clear about what still may sit outside it.

  • PPF / tint cutting software: shops using plotter-cut patterns still need a dedicated pattern system unless DETAILFORGE adds that functionality later.
  • Deep accounting: QuickBooks sync helps, but a CRM is not a replacement for full accounting controls.
  • Specialized marketing stack: built-in campaigns may cover common follow-up, but advanced attribution, ad buying and complex lifecycle automation may still require other tools.
  • Human sales judgment: AI can structure a quote, but high-ticket appearance work often depends on expectation setting, material advice and technical feasibility.

Risks and Unanswered Questions

Because DETAILFORGE is an emerging platform, the unanswered questions matter as much as the feature list.

Vehicle and finish coverage

The value of a configurator depends on whether it handles the cars, finishes, materials and combinations a shop actually sells. The published catalog is encouraging, but every shop should test its own mix.

Render realism versus customer expectation

A visualization can increase conversion, but it can also create expectation risk if the render implies a result that real film, lighting, panel geometry or installation constraints cannot reproduce exactly.

AI quote accuracy

The system uses shop-defined pricing and labor logic, which is the right foundation. Shops should still test edge cases: oversized vehicles, damaged surfaces, removal labor, complex curves, partial panels, bundled services and custom material costs.

Included AI-render limits

Five renders on Solo and 25 on Pro may be enough for testing, but a shop that turns rendering into a high-volume customer acquisition tool should model the pay-as-you-go cost beyond the included allowance.

Integration maturity

Stripe and QuickBooks are useful core integrations. Square is not yet listed as live, and Zapier currently depends on API/webhook paths. Shops with a heavily integrated existing stack should map every required connection before migrating.

Track record

We found a detailed current product site and legal documentation, but not the same depth of long-term public operating history or customer proof available from more established shop platforms. That does not make the product weak; it means the burden of testing is higher.

Who Should Test DETAILFORGE?

STRONG FIT

Wrap / PPF / tint shops selling high-ticket visual upgrades. The 3D and AI-assisted sales workflow is most defensible when the customer needs to choose finishes, coverage or packages before purchasing.

STRONG FIT

Shops with slow manual quoting. If the owner spends hours answering the same pricing and configuration questions, the catalog-driven assistant is worth testing.

POSSIBLE FIT

Multi-service appearance shops. Detailing, coatings, tint, wraps and PPF can live in one system, especially if the business wants online booking and unified customer records.

WEAKER FIT

Simple low-ticket detailing operations. A basic mobile detailer that mainly needs scheduling, invoices and payments may get better value from a cheaper CRM without 3D configuration.

Which DETAILFORGE Plan Makes Sense?

Solo is the right proof-of-concept plan. It gives the shop the core 3D and AI quoting experience for $79/month without a per-user charge.

Pro is the plan we would use for a serious comparison. Online booking, configurator embedding, workflows, inspections, team assignment and margin reporting are the features that determine whether DETAILFORGE can replace multiple tools rather than simply add one more.

Enterprise is specialized. Multi-location control, white-label surfaces and API access are valuable, but most independent shops will not need them.

Final Verdict: Is DETAILFORGE Worth Trying?

Yes — especially for wrap, PPF and tint shops where the quote is part of the sales experience.

The most compelling part of DETAILFORGE is not that it has AI, a CRM or a calendar. Plenty of software has those. The compelling part is the attempt to connect customer imagination, visual configuration, shop-specific pricing and booking in one flow.

If that flow converts even a small number of additional high-ticket jobs — or materially reduces owner time spent on repetitive estimates — the economics can work quickly. But those economics need to be proven inside the individual shop.

The test we would run

Build the shop's five most common high-value services, load the real labor rates and materials, then run 20 realistic quote scenarios. Measure setup time, quote accuracy, customer-facing clarity, render usefulness, deposit conversion and how much manual work still remains. Compare that against the current sales process before migrating the rest of the operation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is DETAILFORGE?

DETAILFORGE is business software for wrap, PPF, tint, ceramic coating and detailing shops. Its headline feature is a 3D vehicle configurator paired with AI-assisted quoting, backed by CRM, scheduling, payments and shop-management tools.

How much does DETAILFORGE cost?

As of September 15, 2026, Solo is $79/month per shop, Pro is $149/month per shop and Enterprise is $279/month per shop. All listed tiers include unlimited users. The vendor advertises a 7-day free trial with no card required.

Does DETAILFORGE include online booking?

Customer self-booking is listed on the Pro plan and above. Solo includes a shop calendar and operational scheduling but not the same customer-facing booking feature set.

Can DETAILFORGE be embedded on a shop website?

The vendor lists website configurator embedding on Pro and above using a line of HTML.

Does DETAILFORGE replace PPF or tint cutting software?

No, not based on the current product positioning we reviewed. DETAILFORGE handles configuration, quoting and operations; dedicated cutting platforms such as OmniPlot focus on patterns, nesting and plotter output.

Is DETAILFORGE better than Tint Wiz?

It depends on the workflow. Tint Wiz has a strong, established film-business feature set around proposals, projects and film inventory. DETAILFORGE is more differentiated for 3D visual configuration and AI-assisted customer quoting.

Is DETAILFORGE better than QuoteIQ?

QuoteIQ starts much cheaper and offers a wide AI toolkit for field-service businesses. DETAILFORGE is more specialized around automotive appearance and visual vehicle configuration. The better choice depends on whether low-cost CRM automation or visual selling is the bigger problem.

Does DETAILFORGE integrate with QuickBooks?

The vendor lists native two-way QuickBooks synchronization for customers, invoices and payments. Stripe supports the core payment flow. Square is listed as coming soon.

Does DETAILFORGE have an affiliate program?

We did not find a public affiliate or referral program on the official DETAILFORGE site during our September 15, 2026 research. Publishers interested in a partnership should ask the vendor directly.
