How to Get More Google Reviews for Your Auto Detailing Business

The hard part usually is not knowing that reviews matter. It is remembering to ask every satisfied customer at the right moment without sounding pushy.

Start with the manual workflow before buying software

If you only complete a handful of jobs each week, keep it simple: finish the detail, confirm the customer is satisfied, then send one short message with your direct Google review link. Track whether the request was sent. If that process is consistent, you may not need another subscription.

The software question starts when the owner or team repeatedly forgets the ask, follow-ups are inconsistent, or customer volume makes manual tracking annoying.

Why detailers lose review opportunities

Recent detailing-community discussions show a familiar pattern: operators say customers are happy, but many never complete a review even when the business sends a thank-you message or a QR code. That is anecdotal evidence, not a universal benchmark, but it matches the operational problem this guide addresses.

The gap is usually between “great service” and “customer actually opens the review page.” A repeatable workflow removes that gap.

A low-friction review workflow

  1. Finish the job and get an explicit quality check. Do not ask for a public review before the customer has had a chance to inspect the result.
  2. Ask once while the experience is fresh. Send a direct review link with a short thank-you.
  3. Use one controlled follow-up. If the customer has not clicked, one reminder a few days later is usually enough for a small shop.
  4. Stop the sequence. More messages are not automatically better. Respect opt-outs and channel preferences.
  5. Measure the workflow. Track jobs completed, requests sent, link clicks if available, and reviews received.

When TrueReview makes sense

TrueReview currently supports individual, bulk and automated review requests by SMS and email, including follow-up sequences and integrations that can trigger a request after events such as a completed appointment or paid transaction. Its site also describes duplicate-contact protections and automated follow-ups.

That makes it relevant when the problem is specifically “we do good work, but we forget to ask consistently.”

Choose the smallest system that solves the problem

If a direct Google link and one manual reminder work, keep them. If the real problem is remembering to ask, a focused reputation tool may be enough. If review requests are only one part of a larger lead-nurture problem, a broader automation platform may be easier to justify.
