How to Set a Mobile Detailing Service Area and Travel Fees

Mobile convenience is part of the product—but a distant low-ticket job can quietly turn a profitable service into hours of unpaid drive time.

Do not price the drive like it is free

Recent mobile-detailing discussions show operators wrestling with the same question: how far should the service radius extend before travel starts destroying the economics of a job? Community answers vary—some use a free local radius, some charge by distance, others use minimum job values or zone-based fees. That variation is the point: there is no universal dollar-per-mile answer.

Start with the economics, not the fee

  1. Define your normal service area. Decide which nearby towns or ZIP codes are included in your base price.
  2. Estimate lost production time. A 90-minute round trip is time you cannot spend detailing another vehicle.
  3. Track fuel, tolls and vehicle cost. Do not look at fuel alone.
  4. Set an outer-zone rule. Choose a surcharge, minimum job value or both.
  5. Cluster work when possible. Multiple vehicles in the same distant area can make the trip work better than one low-ticket job.

A practical policy template

Zone 1: normal service area, travel included.

Zone 2: moderate distance, either a flat surcharge or minimum job value.

Zone 3: distant jobs only when the ticket size, route or grouped bookings justify the drive.

The exact miles and fees should come from your own numbers, not somebody else's Reddit comment.
