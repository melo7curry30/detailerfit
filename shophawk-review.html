What Is ShopHawk?

ShopHawk is business-management software built for automotive restyling and protection shops. The current product targets window tint, paint protection film, ceramic coating, vinyl wrap, auto detailing and related appearance services.

Unlike a generic field-service CRM, ShopHawk connects leads, customers, proposals, work orders, scheduling, invoices, vehicle inspections, inventory and reporting around the same shop workflow. That puts it in the same evaluation set as Tint Wiz, Urable, OrbisX, ShopWrk and other automotive-specific platforms rather than only broad field-service products.

ShopHawk currently publishes four plans. Pricing is per location and every published tier includes unlimited users. The vendor says shops can move up plans as needed.

Starter: useful as a low-cost entry point, but it omits the lead pipeline and most operational features that make ShopHawk different.

Complete: adds the marketing suite, AI features, email/SMS campaigns, review requests and AI booking assistant.

Leads, CRM and Automated Follow-Up

ShopHawk's Growth plan introduces a structured lead pipeline, automated follow-up and quote/estimate tracking. That matters because one of the most common revenue leaks in appearance shops is not generating the quote — it is failing to follow up consistently after the quote.

ShopHawk also emphasizes lead ownership and assignment. That becomes more important as inbound volume increases because a shared inbox without ownership can still lose prospects when everyone assumes somebody else is handling the lead.

Work Orders and Scheduling

Pro adds work orders and scheduling. ShopHawk's public product material shows jobs moving through operational stages instead of living only as calendar appointments. For a multi-bay tint, wrap, PPF or ceramic operation, that distinction matters: management needs to know not only when the vehicle arrives, but where the job sits in production.

3D Vehicle Inspections

One of ShopHawk's more distinctive published features is its 3D vehicle inspection workflow on Pro and Complete. The system is positioned for documenting vehicle condition, flagging issues by area and capturing evidence before installation work begins.

Why this could matter

Pre-existing scratches, chips, paint defects and panel damage can create expensive disputes in PPF, ceramic, tint and wrap work. A structured condition record can improve customer sign-off and give the shop a clearer before-state.

Do not automatically treat this as the same thing as Urable's 3D vehicle visualizer. ShopHawk currently emphasizes inspection and condition documentation; visual sales/configuration is a different job.

Inventory, Commissions and Multi-Location Reporting

Inventory, commissions/upsells and multi-location reporting are listed on Pro and Complete. The combination is especially relevant to a growing group because management can connect sales behavior with shop operations and organization-level KPIs.

However, a generic “inventory” label does not prove film-roll depth. Tint and PPF businesses should ask whether partial rolls, material usage per job, batch/lot information, film cost and low-stock alerts are handled at the level they need.

Commission users should also confirm whether rules can vary by employee, service or profit basis and how refunds, discounts and payroll exports are handled.

Integrations

ShopHawk currently promotes connected workflows with tools including Stripe, Square, QuickBooks, CARFAX, Meta, RingCentral, Twilio, Yelp, Dialpad, Zapier and Google Ads.

Before switching, ask exactly what syncs and in which direction. “Integration” can mean anything from a deep two-way data sync to a lightweight connection. Customers, estimates, invoices, payments, calls, texts, ad leads, accounting categories and inventory should be checked separately.

AI Booking Assistant and Marketing Tools

ShopHawk's Complete plan adds the AI and marketing layer: an AI booking assistant, email and SMS campaigns, review requests and broader AI features.

The strategic question is no longer simply whether a CRM has online booking. It is whether the system can respond to a lead, understand the vehicle and service request, qualify correctly, find an appropriate slot and move the customer into operations without creating another manual handoff.

What to test before trusting the AI

Use messy real-life cases: incomplete vehicle details, discount requests, unsupported film brands, partial PPF coverage, a customer changing vehicles after a quote, multi-day bay reservations, warranty issues and service combinations that conflict with shop rules. AI is useful only if it also knows when to escalate.

Data Migration and Onboarding

ShopHawk's demo page explicitly offers data-migration planning and says the team can discuss moving existing customer and job history into ShopHawk. That is useful, but it is not enough evidence to assume every historical object from another platform will migrate perfectly.

Before cancelling an existing system, test customers, vehicles, historical jobs, estimates, invoices, notes, photos/files, warranties, custom fields and message history. A small sample migration is safer than assuming a full import will preserve everything.

ShopHawk Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Built specifically for automotive restyling and protection shops.
  • Transparent public pricing from $49 to $199 per location.
  • Unlimited users on every published plan.
  • CRM and automated follow-up from Growth.
  • Work orders, scheduling, 3D inspections, inventory and reporting on Pro.
  • Commissions and upsell tracking.
  • Multi-location reporting.
  • AI booking and marketing available on Complete.
  • Supports tint, PPF, wrap, ceramic and detailing in one platform.

Cons / open questions

  • Independent operating track record is still limited.
  • Many of the strongest operational features require Pro at $149/month.
  • AI and marketing require Complete at $199/month.
  • Public inventory documentation is not yet as detailed as some film-focused competitors.
  • AI performance needs real-world edge-case testing.
  • Integration depth should be confirmed individually.
  • Migration completeness is not fully documented publicly.
  • No public affiliate/referral program was found during this review.

Who Should Test ShopHawk?

Put ShopHawk on the demo list if you run tint, PPF, wrap, ceramic and/or detailing, have meaningful lead volume, need clear salesperson ownership, have several technicians or bays, want production stages instead of only appointments, need commission tracking, or are expanding to multiple locations.

Consider a simpler product first if you mainly need basic booking and invoicing, run a very small mobile operation, need extremely deep film-roll inventory or dedicated pattern cutting, or already have a CRM plus AI receptionist stack that works well.

Affiliate / Partnership Opportunity

DetailerFit did not find a public ShopHawk affiliate or referral program during the September 17, 2026 source check. Because ShopHawk is actively publishing competitive comparison pages and appears early in market development, direct publisher/referral outreach is worth testing.

A useful partnership would include a tracked referral link, clear attribution window, fixed or recurring commission, demo access for editorial verification, approved product assets and notice of major pricing or feature changes. Any future commercial relationship should be disclosed clearly and should not change editorial conclusions.

Final Verdict

A serious new contender for growing automotive appearance shops

ShopHawk is more interesting than a generic new CRM because its strongest features map directly to the economics of a restyling operation: lead ownership, follow-up, work orders, commissions, condition documentation, location-level reporting and a path into AI-assisted booking.

The main reason for caution is equally clear: the public evidence is still dominated by ShopHawk's own product and comparison pages. Verify migration, inventory depth, integrations and AI behavior before replacing an established platform.
